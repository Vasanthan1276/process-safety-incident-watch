How to read this watch

1 · Screen and retain

Discovery uses GDELT and Google News RSS. Word-boundary matching and event context reduce business news, technology leaks and unrelated “foundry” hits. Ambiguous reports remain in review. Screened-out historical records stay available under Scope.

2 · Review the evidence

Publisher reputation is a screening aid, never independent verification. Legacy “Confirmed” labels did not establish incident facts. Claims and consequences are source-attributed; missing company, location and event dates remain explicit.

3 · Compare possible duplicates

Matching source URLs may be consolidated. Other matching reports are linked for human review. Shared company, hazard and nearby publication dates do not prove one event. Syndicated coverage is not independent corroboration.

4 · Keep review decisions separate

Draft decisions are saved only in this browser. Export them for review before adding them to config/reviews.json. Publication requires the normal repository review process. Reviewed does not mean every claim is confirmed.

Dates, consequences and lessons

The current window uses incident dates when supplied with evidence. Otherwise it uses publication or discovery dates as a clearly labelled proxy; it cannot establish when the event happened. GDELT “seen” time is discovery time. Source update time and database update time are separate.

Potential fab lessons are prompts for assessment, not findings of root cause, failed safeguards or required corrective actions. No definitive Tier 1 or Tier 2 classification is assigned.

Rules are English-focused and can miss relevant reports. Direct publisher links are shown where available; Google News redirect links remain usable when the original URL cannot be resolved. Missing evidence must be checked at the source.
